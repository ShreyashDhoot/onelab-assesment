# Reconciliation Dataset: Decisions and Quirks

Working notes for the internship assignment: *"A payments company's books don't balance at month end. They know every transaction their platform processed. They know what the bank says actually arrived. The two should match. They don't. Find out why. Show them where the gaps are."*

Generated by `generate_recon_dataset.py` (seed 42). Three outputs: `platform_transactions.csv`, `bank_transactions.csv`, `ground_truth.csv`.

---

## 1. Decisions we made

### Scope and assumptions
| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Currency | INR only, paise precision (2 decimals) |
| Timestamps | All UTC, format `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS` |
| Settlement window | 1-2 calendar days after the platform timestamp |
| Platform rows | Exactly 500 |
| Randomness | Fixed seed (42); two runs give byte-identical files |
| Period | Platform activity 1-30 Nov 2026; bank statement runs to 2 Dec 2026 so month-end items spill over |
| Month-end cut-off | 2026-11-30 23:59:59 UTC |
| Fees | Flat: 2% MDR, plus 18% GST on the fee (no variation by payment method yet) |
| Ground truth | Separate third file, kept apart so the two main files stay realistic |

### Core modelling rule
Platform amount is the **gross** amount the platform expects to receive. A clean bank credit equals it exactly. Anything else (fees, short-pays, typos) is an injected anomaly. So fees appear only in the 12 fee-deduction rows, not on every transaction.

### Schemas
**platform_transactions.csv**: `platform_txn_id, order_id, customer_id, amount_inr, currency, payment_method, txn_type, status, created_at_utc, bank_ref, refund_of_txn_id`

**bank_transactions.csv**: `bank_txn_id, utr, amount_inr, credit_debit, value_date_utc, narration, batch_id, status`

**ground_truth.csv** (one row per record, 995 rows): `group_id, record_source, record_id, record_role, anomaly_category, anomaly_type, is_anomaly, expected_counterpart_ids, related_record_ids, record_amount_inr, group_platform_inr, group_bank_inr, gap_final_inr, gap_at_cutoff_inr, settlement_lag_days, root_cause, expected_resolution`

We added `txn_type` and `refund_of_txn_id` to the platform file (not in the first proposal) so refunds can be represented and linked.

### Where we deviated from the original proposed mix
- Clean rows are 78.4% (392/500), not exactly 80%, because several anomalies span multiple rows (duplicates, batches, refunds with originals).
- We added two cases not in the first list because they follow from the month-end story: **prior-period spillover** (October transactions credited on 1-2 Nov, the mirror image of the cut-off problem) and **chargebacks** that exist only at the bank.
- Duplicates (3 types) and refund cases are slightly larger than the 2% / 1.5% originally suggested.

## 2. Quirks and things to know

### Timing
- **Clean rows use calendar days, weekends included.** A Friday clean transaction can settle on Sunday. This assumes 24x7 rails (UPI, IMPS). The weekend-lag anomaly is the exception: created Fri/Sat, settled on day 3.
- **The last two days of November contain only month-end anomaly rows.** Clean rows are created on 1-28 Nov and other anomalies on 1-27 Nov, so a late-month transaction is never accidentally ambiguous with a genuine missing one. Side effect: volume on 29-30 Nov is lower and entirely tagged.
- **The month-end gap (INR 150,407.55) disappears in the final gap.** That is the point: it is timing only. The final gap (-235,640.94) is the real, unexplained-by-timing difference.
- Month-end rows: created 29-30 Nov; a 29-Nov row uses a 2-day lag so it lands in December.

### Identifiers
- **UTR formats:** UPI and WALLET use 12 digits; CARD and NETBANKING use 16 characters (bank prefix `HDFCR/ICICR/SBINR/AXISR` plus 11 digits).
- **Order ids:** current period `ORD-2026-100000` to `199999`; prior-period items use `90000-99999`, so a low order number in a bank narration signals an October transaction.
- **495 unique order ids across 500 platform rows**, by design: 3 webhook-retry duplicates and 2 refund rows reuse an existing order id.
- **Orphan refunds reference ghost ids** `PTX0009001` and `PTX0009002`, which sit outside the real id range (`PTX0000001-0000500`) and exist in no file.
- Platform and bank ids (`PTX0000001...`, `BNK0000001...`) are assigned after sorting by timestamp, and the ground truth uses these final ids.
- **REF_FORMAT_MISMATCH keeps whitespace on purpose.** Alphanumeric refs are lower-cased with a trailing space; UPI refs get a `UTR-` prefix and spaces. Do not auto-strip when loading if you want to test normalisation.
- Webhook-duplicate platform rows carry the **same** `bank_ref` as the original, which is what makes them detectable.

### Sign and status conventions
- **Platform refunds are negative; bank refunds are positive with `credit_debit = DEBIT`.** Matching must normalise signs.
- Only `SUCCESS` rows count toward cash in the gap calculation. A `FAILED` or `REVERSED` bank row and a `FAILED` or `PENDING` platform row contribute zero.
- `REVERSED` is modelled as a single bank row with status `REVERSED`, not as a credit plus a separate debit row.
- Platform `FAILED` and `PENDING` rows have an empty `bank_ref`, but the bank narration still carries the order id.

### Amount anomalies
- Fee = round-half-up(2% of amount); GST = round-half-up(18% of fee); bank credit = amount - fee - GST. Fee rows use amounts of INR 500-49,999.
- Rounding rows differ by +/-1 or 2 paise; the net across the three rows is -0.02.
- Wrong-amount rows are digit transpositions on amounts of INR 1,000-49,999 (e.g. 18,540 becomes 15,840), with paise preserved. Both landed with the bank higher, giving -57,600.
- Partial settlements pay 50-90% of the amount; the remainder never arrives.

### Hard-to-match cases
- **Batches:** platform rows have no `bank_ref`; the bank row has a `batch_id` and the **exact gross sum** of the members (no fee deducted at batch level). Members share a payment method and a 1-2 day creation span.
- **Same-amount-same-day:** two UPI orders with identical amounts, created hours apart, with bank postings in **reverse order**. UTR or order id resolves them; amount plus date alone does not. The ground truth overrides `expected_counterpart_ids` with the specific correct pairing.
- **REF_MISSING:** no `bank_ref` and no order id in the narration; only amount (non-round, with paise, to keep it unique) plus the settlement window can pair them.

### Ground truth behaviour
- `is_anomaly` is False only for `CLEAN_MATCH`. Special cases that net to zero (processed refunds, batches, same-amount pairs, reference problems) are still marked True because they are matching challenges.
- The original payments behind refunds and chargebacks are labelled `CLEAN_MATCH`; the refund or chargeback group links back via `related_record_ids`.
- For platform-side rows `expected_counterpart_ids` lists the bank rows of the group, and vice versa (default), except where overridden as above.
- `settlement_lag_days` = earliest bank date minus latest platform date in the group; blank when one side is absent.
- Missing-in-bank rows were created on 1-27 Nov, so they are far enough from the cut-off that "not yet settled" is not a valid explanation.

## 3. Verification done
- Script's built-in asserts: 500 platform rows, unique ids, every record appears in ground truth exactly once, clean rows have lag 1-2, group gaps sum to the direct total.
- Independent re-read of the CSVs: clean lag distribution 249 at 1 day and 143 at 2 days; weekend-lag rows all at 3 days; all 20 month-end bank rows in December; sign conventions as described above.
- Naive exact-UTR matcher: 463 correct, 0 wrong, 37 unmatched. The 37 are exactly the missing-in-bank (15), batch (8), reference-problem (10) and status-mismatch rows with no reference (4), so no hidden matching errors.
- Determinism confirmed by comparing checksums of two runs.

## 4. Not modelled (possible extensions)
- Currency conversion or non-INR transactions.
- Fees varying by payment method (currently flat 2% + 18% GST).
- Batch-level fee deduction (batches settle gross).
- A real bank holiday calendar (weekend lag is a simple rule).
- Multiple merchants or accounts, and bank charges on their own statement lines.
- Partial refunds of batched transactions; refunds appearing in the bank in a different month than the platform.

## 5. How to regenerate
```bash
python generate_recon_dataset.py                       # seed 42, current folder
python generate_recon_dataset.py --seed 7 --out-dir out/   # different dataset
```
The scenario counts live in `SCENARIO_COUNTS` at the top of the script; clean rows automatically fill the remainder up to 500.
